ALCHEMISTS: real proof of work, on Robinhood Chain

A mine that issues a new challenge every minute. You hash it with your address, keep the best hash of the minute, and submit it in the next one. The hash becomes an ingredient: one of 40 types, in one of 5 tiers. Nobody picks what they get, and nobody knows what it is until the reveal. That is the whole game, and everything else grows out of it.

HOW THE MINE WORKS

Every minute has a bar, a number of leading zero bits a hash must clear. The bar moves with the network: every two minutes, at most two bits at a time, inside a corridor of 30 to 43 bits. More hashrate, higher bar. Clear the bar and you submit. Clear it by 2 bits and the ingredient is Uncommon, by 4 Rare, by 7 Epic, by 10 Legendary. One submit per address per minute, no exceptions.

The ingredient stays sealed until the next minute's challenge exists. Then it is revealed: type, tier and a 1-in-16 upgrade roll are all decided by a challenge that did not exist when you mined. There is no way to mine for a specific ingredient, and no way to know what you are getting before you pay.

Rarities unlock as the network grows: Uncommon, Rare, Epic and Legendary open one after another as the estimated hashrate rises, permanently. A cold network mints only Common, however lucky the hash.

You can mine in the browser on a CPU, in the browser on a GPU through WebGPU, or with a standalone CUDA miner on a rig with up to 32 addresses per card. Sign submits with a session wallet that lives in your browser, or with your own wallet, one confirmation a minute. Nothing is deposited anywhere.

THE WORKSHOP

Ingredients are raw material. Two herbs of one tier brew into a potion. A furnace, built from ingredients of its own tier, refines: ten ingredients of one type and tier plus a potion go in, and with a 90 to 50 percent chance depending on tier, one ingredient of the next tier comes out. Fail, and everything is lost. A hotter furnace adds five points.

The crucible melts ten ingredients of a tier into five of the same tier, random types, with a 5 percent roll for a tier up on one of them and, at higher tiers, a growing risk of a tier down. Choosing the output category costs pieces.

The ritual table seals five ingredients by recipe into an item: a grimoire, a candle, a chalice, a seal, a scepter, a censer, a mirror, a relic. Eight kinds, five tiers, each with a chance to come out one tier higher. These items are what the summoning will ask for.

THE CAULDRON

Every submit pays a small price in ETH. It starts low and grows with the square root of everything mined, and falls back as ingredients are burned in the workshop. All of it flows into the Cauldron: a Safe multisig behind a 48-hour timelock, on chain, readable by anyone at any time.

Once the alchemists are summoned, 60 percent of the Cauldron streams daily to their holders, weighted by rarity and by how early they were summoned. The other 40 percent thickens.

THE KEYS

Twenty-one mythic keys exist, and only twenty-one. Each is the signature belonging of a named alchemist: the Emerald Tablet, Azoth, the Staff of Merlin, the Obsidian Mirror, and seventeen more. A key drops from a lucky reveal or a lucky craft while any remain, and in the summoning a key in its slot yields the named one-of-one alchemist.

THE OPENING ACT

Mining, the workshop, the summoning: this is the first act. Every line of it leads to the ritual of the Philosopher's Stone. What the ritual asks for, and what the Stone is, is not written anywhere. Not in the contracts, not on the site. Yet.
